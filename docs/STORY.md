# Runik - Story Bible

## Premise

Jarnvik, a mining town at the roots of a glacier, in the year the sun dimmed.
The Runewrights' Guild once kept the town alive: machines of inscribed stone
that sorted, fused and ferried gems from the deep seams. Now the guild is one
old master and one apprentice - and then the master vanishes.

**Eir Runasdottir** (player) - last apprentice. Practical, stubborn, learns
by doing. The game's tutorial voice is her working through Alviss' journals.

**Master Alviss** (absent) - the last full Runewright. Left five journals and
a note: *"The stone the sky forgot. I have gone to fetch it."*

## The Two Courts

The world is doubled. The **Gold Court** (Material plane) is the day-world:
iron, frost, weight, runes that burn like embers, wheels that turn sunwise.
The **Azure Court** (Ethereal plane) lies beneath it like a reflection in
still water: weightless, cold-lit, wheels turning widdershins. Dagaz - the
day-rune, the door-rune - is the only mark that opens the way between them.

The courts are not metaphors. Alviss crossed over physically, chasing the
**Celestial Aetherstone** - a gem said to have fallen from the sky before
the sky forgot it, that exists in both courts at once. He found the way in
but not the way back: the door home must be *fed*, and what it eats is the
very stone he went to find. It cannot be found - only **composed**: sea,
light and moon (sapphire, diamond, moonstone) fused in the day-world and
quenched in the night-world. Five times, for the five points of the door.

## Chapter arc

1. **First Marks** - Eir reopens the training hall. Need (Nauthiz), the thorn
   (Thurisaz), the journey (Raidho). The machines answer her for the first
   time.
2. **The Two Courts** - the thin place behind the forge. First crossing;
   runes glow blue; the wheel turns the other way. The Loom teaches that the
   courts trade, and that a runewright is the loom.
3. **Order of Precedence** - the guild cellar, where century-old machines
   still run. Rank: the order in which the world listens. A rune may move a
   rune, as a king moves a jarl.
4. **Sundering and Union** - Gebo, the gift given both ways; Kenaz, the
   torch that opens. The burned page: the Aetherstone is not found but made.
5. **The Aetherstone** - the forge relit. Gold and azure, wheel and door,
   and the five hexes drawn in a careful, hopeful hand. The final machine
   forges five Aetherstones; the door opens; Alviss comes home to a town
   whose machines are running again.

## Epilogue (shown after c5p3)

The door takes the stones one by one, like coins into a ferryman's palm.
On the fifth it opens - not onto the Azure Court, but onto the workshop
itself, seen from the other side. Alviss steps through, older by a winter
that never happened, and looks a long time at the machine still turning
behind her.

"You kept the wheel turning," he says. "That is the whole craft. The rest
is decoration."

## Naming notes

- Rune meanings follow the Elder Futhark: Laguz (water/lake, that which
  draws), Thurisaz (giant's force), Raidho (ride/journey), Gebo (gift),
  Kenaz (torch), Dagaz (day, transformation).
- "Sunwise / widdershins" - traditional terms for clockwise / counter-
  clockwise ritual motion; they encode the plane-dependent orbit direction.
